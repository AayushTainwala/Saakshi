# High Court review: BSA s.63 electronic-records certificate (Schedule Part A / Part B)

Source set: the 98 High Court results from four Indian Kanoon searches ("63(4)" / "63(4)(c)" / "section 63" with "Bharatiya Sakshya", and "Part B", "hash value" or "certificate", restricted to High Courts), fetched and read on 2026-10-20. This was **not an exhaustive search**: SCC Online and Manupatra were not available. Linked from `docs/research/law.md`, "Results of the checks", item 6. Every proposition below comes from the fetched text; quotes are verbatim (line breaks joined; "..." marks an omission). Paragraph numbers are as printed in the judgment. "IEA" = Indian Evidence Act, 1872.

**Headline findings**

- **Topic (a), Part B signatory / s.79A examiner:** none of the 98 documents decides whether the Part B "expert" must be an Examiner of Electronic Evidence notified under IT Act s.79A. None of them mentions s.79A in the BSA context. None cites Madras HC *R v. B* (2024 SCC OnLine Mad 6084, CRP(MD) 2362/2024) or SC *Pune Bar Association v. Union of India* (22.05.2026). Searches for "79A", "Examiner of Electronic", "6084", "2362", "Pune Bar" found nothing relevant. The only Bombay hits come from the 7/11 train-blasts judgment, which is an IEA case and not about s.79A. The material that does touch Part B is procedural: Delhi orders give time for, or exempt a party from, the "Certificate of the Expert". One Madras order allowed a certificate issued by a private data-transfer firm, without discussing whether that firm was competent to issue it.
- **No conflict between High Courts on (a)** can be shown from this set (see section 3).
- **There is tension on (c), late or cured certificates:** Rajasthan HC (*Kana Ram*) and one Gujarat bench (*Parag Vaniya*) take a strict line. Allahabad, a different Gujarat bench, P&H, Madras and Delhi allow late or fresh certificates.

## 3. Conflicts between High Courts

**Topic (a) (Part B signatory: s.79A Examiner or any expert):** no conflict can be shown from this set. No High Court in the 98 documents decides the question.

- Delhi HC orders (*Ashutosh Gupta*, *Chalet Hotels*, *Hershey*) refer to Part B as the "Certificate of the Expert". They grant time for it or exempt the party from it, but never say who qualifies, and never mention s.79A.
- Madras HC (*K. Revathi*, 17.02.2026) accepted, subject only to adding the IMEI, a certificate issued by a private firm that transferred the data. The competence of that firm was neither raised nor decided. It does not cite *R v. B*, which is also a Madras HC decision.
- Nothing here follows or departs from *R v. B* (Mad 2024) or *Pune Bar Association* (SC, 22.05.2026). This review cannot say how those decisions have been received by other High Courts. A targeted search ("79A", "Examiner of Electronic Evidence", "Part B" + "Sakshya") would be needed.

**Related tension on topic (c) (not asked, but relevant):**

- *Strict:* Rajasthan HC, *Kana Ram* (17.04.2026, reportable, under IEA s.65B with a forward-looking direction mentioning BSA s.63): certificates must be contemporaneous, and a late certificate is "not a curative tool". Gujarat HC, *Parag Vaniya* (03.08.2026, under IEA s.65B): the certificate "must accompany" the record and "cannot be bypassed".
- *Lenient:* Allahabad HC, *Chandra Kant* (25.05.2026), following *Khotkar* paras 56 and 59 via *T. Naseer*; Gujarat HC, *Shreeji Enterprise* (01.04.2026), allowing a certificate at the appellate stage and calling the requirement "procedural"; P&H HC, *Amrik Singh* (08.12.2025); Madras HC, *K. Revathi*; MP HC, *Yogesh Markam*; Delhi HC's routine extensions.
- Two benches of the Gujarat HC thus point in different directions within months. *Kana Ram* does not discuss *Khotkar* paras 56–61 in the text fetched.
- *Shreeji*'s description of s.65B(4) as "procedural in nature" (para 13) differs from HP HC, *Nirmal Malakar* (para 9: "not a mere procedural requirement"). HP does not address timing.
