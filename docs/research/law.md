# Protestor Assist App: primary-source legal research (India)

Research date: 16 October 2026. Only primary texts (statutes and judgments) are cited as authority.

## How the sources were accessed (read this first)

- **India Code, eGazette, MHA, sci.gov.in (api/main/digiscr) could not be reached.** From this research environment they were either blocked at the network proxy or refused automated fetching (robots.txt). I could not open any official statute PDF or official SC judgment PDF.
- **Statute text was therefore verified from Indian Kanoon reproductions.** Indian Kanoon itself carries a warning on the BNSS, BNS and DPDP pages: "The original publication document is not available and this content could not be verified." For BNSS and BNS, "VERIFIED" below means *verified from the Indian Kanoon reproduction of the bare text*. Before anything goes into the app, cross-check against the gazette text on India Code or eGazette.
- **Judgments were read in full on Indian Kanoon.** Indian Kanoon reproduces the judgment text as uploaded by the court. Paragraph numbers are given as they appear in that text. Where the Indian Kanoon text is unnumbered (Anvar, D.K. Basu, Arnesh), I say so.
- **The BSA Schedule (the certificate) is not reproduced on Indian Kanoon.** I verified its fields from two sources:
  - a **State Government Gazette reproduction**: Gazette of Puducherry, Extraordinary Part II, No. 40, 9 May 2025, G.O. Ms. No. 27 (Home), pp. 720–723;
  - the Supreme Court's description of the certificate in *Pune Bar Association* (2026).

  The PRS-hosted copy of Act 47 of 2023 confirmed the text of s.63(4), but its Schedule pages were not readable through my fetch tool. Status for the Schedule is therefore "VERIFIED from a government gazette reproduction; central Gazette not checked".
- Format: Proposition | Provision/para | Short quote | Link | Status.

### 1B. BSA Schedule: certificate under s.63(4)(c), field list

Source: Gazette of Puducherry, Extraordinary Part II No. 40, 9 May 2025 (G.O. Ms. No. 27, Home Dept., 6 May 2025), "Comprehensive Guidelines for the admissibility of digital and electronic records under the BSA, 2023", Schedule at pp. 720–723.
Link: https://styandptg.py.gov.in/2025/MAY/EXTRAORDINARYPART-II/1/40-PART-II%20dated%2009-05-2025.pdf

**Status:** VERIFIED from this state government gazette reproduction, which is headed "CERTIFICATE [See section 63(4)(c)]". **Not verified against the central Gazette of India text.** The fetch tool returned line-by-line quotes. A human should check punctuation and line breaks against the Act PDF before reproducing the form in-app.

**PART A (To be filled by the Party)**
1. "I, ............ (Name), son/daughter/spouse of ............ residing/employed at ............ do hereby solemnly affirm and sincerely state and submit as follows:–"
2. "I have produced electronic record/output of the digital record taken from the following device/digital record source (tick mark):–"
   - Computer/Storage Media / DVR / Mobile / Flash Drive / CD/DVD / Server / Cloud / Other / "Other: ............"
   - "Make and Model:" / "Color:" / "Serial Number:" / "IMEI/UIN/UID/MAC/Cloud ID ............ (as applicable)"
   - "and any other relevant information, if any, about the device/digital record ............ (specify)."
3. The fetch tool returned the declaration in fragments, which I have joined:
   > "The digital device or the digital record source was under the lawful control for regularly creating, storing or processing information for the purposes of carrying out regular activities and during this period, the computer or the communication device was working properly and the relevant information was regularly fed into the computer during the ordinary course of business. If the computer/digital device at any point of time was not working properly or out of operation, then it has not affected the electronic/digital record or its accuracy."
4. "The digital device or the source of the digital record is:– Owned / Maintained / Managed / Operated by me (select as applicable)."
5. **"I state that the HASH value/s of the electronic/digital record/s is ............, obtained through the following algorithm:– SHA1: / SHA256: / MD5: / Other ............ (Legally acceptable standard)"**
6. **"(Hash report to be enclosed with the certificate)"**
7. "(Name and signature)" / "Date (DD/MM/YYYY):" / "Time (IST): ...... hours (In 24 hours format)" / "Place:"

**PART B (To be filled by the Expert)**
1. Same identity block: "I, ...... (Name), son/daughter/spouse of ...... residing/employed at ...... do hereby solemnly affirm and sincerely state and submit as follows:–"
2. "The produced electronic record/output of the digital record are obtained from the following device/digital record source (tick mark):–" followed by the same source list and the same Make/Model, Color, Serial Number, IMEI/UIN/UID/MAC/Cloud ID and "other relevant information" fields.
3. **"I state that the HASH value/s of the electronic/digital record/s is ......, obtained through the following algorithm:– SHA1: / SHA256: / MD5: / Other ...... (Legally acceptable standard)"**
4. **"(Hash report to be enclosed with the certificate)"**
5. "(Name, designation and signature)" / "Date (DD/MM/YYYY):" / "Time (IST): ... hours (In 24 hours format)" / "Place:"

**Answer to the hash question.** Yes, both Part A and Part B require the hash value(s) of the record. The form names **SHA1, SHA256 and MD5** and also allows "Other (Legally acceptable standard)". A hash report must be enclosed. Part B is the expert's own declaration of the source device and the hash. It contains no s.63(2)-style "lawful control" declaration. The form does not mandate a specific algorithm.

For app design: compute SHA-256 at capture, and preferably also MD5 and SHA-1, since the form lists all three. Generate a hash report. Pre-fill the Part A device fields (make, model, serial number, IMEI where the OS permits, time in IST 24-hour format). Part B still needs a human expert; the app cannot supply it (see *Pune Bar Association* below).

### 2E. Is there a right to video-record police doing public duties in public?

- **No statute and no Supreme Court judgment I could find expressly confers or recognises such a right.** I am saying this plainly: there is no direct primary authority.
- **Statutes do point towards recording:** BNSS s.105 and s.185(2) proviso make *police* record searches and seizures on audio-video, "preferably mobile phone". That obligation is on the police; it is not a citizen's right. *(VERIFIED, IK)*
- **Narrow Bombay HC authority:** *Ravindra Shitalrao Upadhyay v. State of Maharashtra*, Crl. Appln. (APL) 615/2021, Nagpur Bench (Manish Pitale & Valmiki SA Menezes JJ.), 26.07.2022, paras 10–12. The court quashed an Official Secrets Act s.3 FIR for video-recording inside a police station, because a police station is not a "prohibited place" under OSA s.2(8): "It is an exhaustive definition, which does not specifically include Police Station" (para 10). Link: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/134007964/. *(VERIFIED, IK)* This shows one offence does not apply. It does **not** establish a general right to record.
- **Practical gap for the app:** recording is lawful by default, since no statute prohibits it in public. The risk lies in charges for obstruction or disobedience if the recording interferes (BNS ss.221, 223, 132; see 2F). The app should say "record from a distance; do not obstruct". It should not say "you have a legal right to film police".

> 37. Acts against which there is no right of private defence.
> (1) There is no right of private defence,—
> (a) against an act which does not reasonably cause the apprehension of death or of grievous hurt, if done, or attempted to be done, by a public servant acting in good faith under colour of his office, though that act, may not be strictly justifiable by law;
> (b) against an act which does not reasonably cause the apprehension of death or of grievous hurt, if done, or attempted to be done, by the direction of a public servant acting in good faith under colour of his office, though that direction may not be strictly justifiable by law;
> (c) in cases in which there is time to have recourse to the protection of the public authorities.
> (2) The right of private defence in no case extends to the inflicting of more harm than it is necessary to inflict for the purpose of defence.
> Explanation 1.—A person is not deprived of the right of private defence against an act done, or attempted to be done, by a public servant, as such, unless he knows or has reason to believe, that the person doing the act is such public servant.
> Explanation 2.—A person is not deprived of the right of private defence against an act done, or attempted to be done, by the direction of a public servant, unless he knows, or has reason to believe, that the person doing the act is acting by such direction, or unless such person states the authority under which he acts, or if he has authority in writing, unless he produces such authority, if demanded.

(Line breaks added; Indian Kanoon runs the clauses together.)

**Correction to the earlier summary.** Stating or producing authority appears only in **Explanation 2**, which is about a person acting *by the direction of* a public servant. For an act by the public servant themself, Explanation 1 turns only on whether the person knows, or has reason to believe, that the actor is a public servant. The earlier summary, and SPEC section 6 ("Explanations 1 and 2 keep the right where the person is not known to be a public servant, or does not state or produce their authority when asked"), read the authority condition across both Explanations. The section does not say that a public servant who does not state or produce their authority loses its protection.

**Application to the app (analysis, not authority):**
- **On-device only** (contacts and location never leave the phone, and the developer has no access):
  - The developer arguably does not "process" the data, because the storage is the user's own.
  - The user storing their own emergency contacts is arguably within the s.3(c)(i) personal/domestic exemption.
